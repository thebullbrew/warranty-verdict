The Method

A warranty claim has three outcomes, and the brochure only mentions one. Approved: you pay the service fee, plus anything above the per-claim cap. Denied or short-paid: you pay the fee's cousin — the full contractor bill — after weeks of waiting. Either way: you pay in hours, because their contractor works on their schedule, not yours.

This tool prices all three, every year of the hold, and sets the total against the unglamorous alternative: keep the premium, fund a reserve, hire your own contractor in one phone call. Self-insuring still costs an hour of scheduling per repair — that is counted too.

The honest exception: a warranty can be worth it in year one of owning an old house with tired systems and an empty reserve — as cash-flow insurance, not as a bargain. If the math says buy it, buy it with your eyes open, and re-run this the day a major system gets replaced. New systems are the real warranty.
